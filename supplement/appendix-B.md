### Result comparisons: Result Comparisons and Prior Foundations (Full Version)

**Caption:** The framework adapts classical causal and off-policy tools to agent interventions with declared stopping and replacement semantics. Each row states the closest prior work and the agent-specific formulation under the hypotheses of the formal result.

| Result | Closest prior work | What is new here |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Display (2); Proposition A.4.1: agreement and stopping replay | g-formula and censoring at deviation (Robins, 1986; Cain et al., 2010); stopping rules evaluated from full-length trajectories (Goel et al., 2017) | Under (R), (A1) to (A3): agreement paths retain logged laws; declared abort valuations identify stopping loss even if the baseline never aborts. |
| Proposition 3.1: logging sufficiency | A log determines an expectation for every law exactly when paths it cannot tell apart have equal values; information states (Subramanian et al., 2020) | Under (R), (A1), (A2): logged contributions and unresolved mass are identified for every baseline law if and only if both are constant on log fibers. |
| Proposition E.1.1 (`prop:replay-cont`); display (5): continuing deviations | Worst-case bounds (Manski, 2003); bounds for dynamic regimes under confounding (Zhang et al., 2020) | Under (R), (A1) to (A3) with full records: continuing terms vary over prefix-feasible sets, with extrema attained under (S) and outer-bounded otherwise. |
| Corollary E.7.1: long horizons | Chain rule for survival probabilities; worst-case bounds (Manski, 2003) | Under (R), (A1), and (A2), agreement mass is the product of one minus each deviation hazard. With full records, (A3), (S), only continuing deviations, and every feasible loss set having spread at least $\delta$, the identified mean interval has width at least $\delta[1-P^\kappa(\mathsf A_\kappa\mid r)]$. |
| Proposition A.5.1, first part: supported continuation | Sequential g-formula (Robins, 1986; Pearl, 1995b) | Under (A1), (A2), (G1) to (G4): sequential g-formula identifies continuation laws without requiring support at inputs where the specification fixes kernel values. |
| Proposition A.5.1, second part; Proposition F.5.2: unsupported inputs | Sharp bounds for dynamic regimes (Alvarez & Ferrando, 2026); polynomial programs for discrete causal models (Duarte et al., 2023) | Under (A1), (A2), (G2) to (G4) without (G1): g-formula image over free baseline-null kernels gives sharp bounds, or outer bounds when kernels are shared. |
| Corollary F.6.1: model updates | Logged replay (Li et al., 2011) | Under (A1), (A2) with recorded inputs and random seeds: seed-addressable new policies identify agreement mass without randomized logging, bounded by first-decision agreement. |

### Closest Work (Extended Positioning)

*The following positioning text appeared originally in Appendix B.4 and has been moved to this companion file, as substantive comparative discussions are maintained in Appendix C.*

> PRA for AI permits Bayesian networks parameterized by empirical rates such as red-team bypass frequencies (Wisakanto et al., 2025, Sections 3.2, 4.3, and 5.2), but provides no replacement semantics for logged runs. AI Control tests protocols across resampled outputs and monitor scores against an adaptive red team (Greenblatt et al., 2024, Sections 2.2 and 2.5). Ctrl-Z assesses stateful resampling protocols from score distributions (Bhatt et al., 2025, Sections 2.2 and 3.2.2, Appendix C); reusing its measurements under altered routing requires baseline support at newly reached histories (Proposition A.5.1). Our framework states the assumptions that replay requires and distinguishes conditions the record law can establish from declarations and invariance assumptions it cannot verify.
