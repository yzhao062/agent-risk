## Budget Checks

### Accept Region: Realized Pool Cutoff (Companion Material for Appendix F.8)

**Accept Region of Proposition F.7.1.** Among $n$ episodes, $b$ are resolved and harmful and $N$ are unresolved (Appendix F.7). Let $m$, the unresolved mass, be the probability that an episode is unresolved. It is at most the continuing mass of Lemma F.7.2, which also counts prefixes with harm before the deviation. Let $\gamma$ bound the total-variation restoration error at every unresolved prefix, declare $m_0\ge m$ before reading the log, and put $\xi_0=m_0\gamma$ (Remark F.7.3). The design certifies when $u_{\mathrm{CP}}(S^U,n;a_L)+\xi_0\le q^\star$. By Clopper-Pearson duality this means $S^U\le c$. Here $c$ is the largest integer $z$ with $\Pr(\mathrm{Bin}(n,q^\star-\xi_0)\le z)\le a_L$, and $c=-1$ if none exists or $q^\star\le\xi_0$. With one look, let $X$ count the harms in a uniform sample of $k$ of the $N$ unresolved episodes, and put $d=c-b$. The design never certifies if $d<0$ and always certifies if $d\ge N$. Otherwise it certifies if and only if $X\le x_k$, where $x_k=\max\{x\ge-1:\Pr_{\mathrm H(N,d+1,k)}(X\le x)\le e\}$ and $x_k=-1$ means never. Given the restored cohort, with $D$ harmful unresolved episodes, the last case certifies with probability $\Pr_{\mathrm H(N,D,k)}(X\le x_k)$. Under (H) the design certifies with probability at most $a_L+e$ (Proposition F.7.1(ii) and Remark F.7.3). The bound averages over the log and the sample, and holds when the live harm exceeds $q^\star$ and the restoration error is at most $\gamma$. Using the realized $N/n$ in place of $m_0$ lets the cutoff depend on the log. The certification probability at live harm just above $q^\star$ can then exceed $a_L$. At E1-like values with $\gamma=0.1476$, $k=N$, and a multinomial log, it is at least 0.027 for live harm within $10^{-5}$ of $q^\star$. It tends to 0.028 as the live harm falls to $q^\star$.

### E1 Check: Fixed-Sample and Live Comparisons (Post Hoc, Companion Material for Appendix F.8)

**E1 Check.** At the E1 values ($n=1{,}200$, $N=479$, $b=27$, $D=8$), $m=0.399$, $\zeta=0.0708$, and $\bar p=0.194$ at $\gamma=0$. For $\alpha=0.05$, $\beta=0.2$, and $\gamma=0$, the bounds are 12.3 executions and 50.7 live episodes. The ratio of their quadratic forms, $m^2\bar p(1-\bar p)/\{q^\star(1-q^\star)\}$, is 0.28. The smallest fixed-sample tests that certify when the harm count falls below a cutoff use 23 executions and 76 live episodes (17 and 74 with randomization at the cutoff). Part (iii) asks for validity under every live law with harm above $q^\star$. A live test that also trusts the log's $q_0$ and $m$ need watch only the unresolved episodes; one such test averages 45.1 live episodes at E1. D1 averages 46.6 executions, at higher power and with a finite log ($c=99$ against $nq^\star=120$).
The budget design table (`tab:budget`, archived in [supplement/appendix-D.md#budget-design-table](appendix-D.md#budget-design-table)) sets D1 against a live test of power 0.806 at the live rate 0.030. Both meet one-sided 95% coverage and power 0.80, and D1 exceeds the minimum. Its power of 0.99963 is given the log and its cohort. Over a random log in $W_1$, D1 has power 0.993 with 45.1 executions. A fixed-sample live test on the harm count with that power needs 191 episodes at the live rate 0.030 and 179 at 0.0292. The agent-call ratio is then 0.17 and 0.19, against the 0.45 of the table. Necessity begins at $\gamma=\zeta/m=0.177$, and the D1 tolerance (Restoration Allowance in supplement/appendix-D.md) is 0.148. The pool of 479 executions already limits every procedure to $\gamma\le0.159$ (E1 Achievability Check below). The rest of the gap is the sampling margin of the log and, slightly, the continuing mass used there.

### E1 Achievability Check: Threshold Grids (Post Hoc, Companion Material for Appendix F.8)

**E1 Achievability Check.** Take $N=479$, $p=8/479$, $\bar p=93/479$ ($\gamma=0$), $q=35/1200$, $\alpha=0.05$, and $\beta=0.2$. Exact dynamic programming on the lattice of trials and harms gives the laws, given the realized log. With Ville thresholds $(\log20,\log5)$, the execution test has size $0.038$, power $0.899$, and $17.0$ expected executions, between $12.3$ and $\bar K_{\mathrm{ex}}=20.6$. The proposition guarantees only power $1-\beta-\chi_n=0.714$ for these thresholds. The live test has size $0.042$, power $0.874$, and $63.7$ expected episodes, between $50.7$ and $81.7$. At these error levels the sandwiches are factors $1.7$ and $1.6$ wide.

A grid of thresholds $(A,B)$ searches the natural family (Wald and Wolfowitz, 1948), with $181\times150$ pairs of step $0.02$ for executions and $71\times57$ of step $0.05$ for live episodes. Size is evaluated at the boundary rate (Proposition F.8.2(i)). The best pair with size at most $0.05$ and power at least $0.80$ uses $12.7$ executions (size $0.049$, power $0.801$), $3.4\%$ above the bound. The best live pair uses $53.5$ episodes (size $0.048$, power $0.815$). Averaging over the log changes the execution values by less than $10^{-5}$. These pairs are valid and powerful procedures, so their costs bound $C_{\mathrm{ex}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{lv}}$ from above. Their ratio is $0.238$, against $\mathrm{kl}_{\mathrm{lv}}/\mathrm{kl}_{\mathrm{ex}}=0.243$ and the $0.28$ of the quadratic forms above. All three count executions and episodes, not agent calls.

With all $479$ executions, the most powerful test of size $0.05$ reaches power $0.80$ at $\gamma=0.159$, so no procedure with this pool is powerful beyond it. The D1 tolerance is $0.148$, and necessity begins at $\zeta/m=0.177$. Clopper-Pearson limits from the realized log at $\alpha_{\log}=0.01$ give $\bar p^U=0.147$. With $\alpha=0.04$, a total error of $0.05$, the Ville test then uses $26.4$ executions at power $0.896$, given the log. It uses $17.0$ at power $0.899$ when $q_0$ and $m$ are known.

### Estimated Log Masses

A finite log gives only $b/n$ and $N/n$, and $\bar p$ decreases in $q_0$ and in $m$. Let $q_0^U\ge q_0$ and $m^U\ge m$ be Clopper-Pearson upper limits at level $\alpha_{\log}/2$ each, so that both hold with probability at least $1-\alpha_{\log}$. Run the execution test with $\bar p^U=(q^\star-q_0^U)/m^U-\gamma$ in place of $\bar p$ and the realized $N$ as pool, and require $p<\bar p^U<1$ before evaluating the test, never certifying when this condition fails. On the event of the limits, $\bar p^U\le\bar p<\hat p$, so the size is at most $\alpha+\alpha_{\log}$. Given a log with $p<\bar p^U<1$, the proof of (ii) gives $\mathbb E[K\mid\mathrm{log}]\le\bar K^U$ and failure probability at most $\beta'+\bar K^U/N$, where $\bar K^U$ is $\bar K_{\mathrm{ex}}$ at $\bar p^U$. Unconditionally, the power loses at most $\mathbb P(\bar p^U\le p\text{ or }\bar p^U\ge1)+\mathbb E[\min\{1,\bar K^U/N\};p<\bar p^U<1]$. Both terms vanish as $n$ grows, because $\bar p^U\to\bar p\in(p,1)$ and $N\to\infty$ in probability.

## Strata Checks

### Allocation, Pools, and Snapshot Scope

The shares $a$ are part of the test's validity, not only of its cost. In the instance of `verify_budget_strata.py`, the same increments with draws in proportion to $w_s$ have a per-draw mean of $e^Z$ above one at a boundary point of $\mathcal A$, and their size there is about 0.15 at $\alpha=0.05$. Design values $\tilde p\in(0,1)^S$ from a pilot on separate episodes, such as smoothed proportions, keep the size at $\alpha$ by part (i), with no certification when $\zeta'\le0$ at $\tilde p$; we do not analyze the cost of such an adaptive rule (compare Garivier and Kaufmann, 2016). The pools are random, as in Appendix F.8: given the log, the unexecuted outcomes of a stratum are independent Bernoulli draws. When the target is the harm fraction of a fixed cohort, the lower bound need not hold. With $S=1$, $q_0=0$, $m=c_1=1$, $\gamma=0$, $q^\star=0.5$, and 49 harmful among 100 unresolved episodes, executing all 100 decides that fraction without error, whereas $\mathrm{kl}(0.95,0.05)/\mathrm{kl}(0.49,0.5)>13{,}000$. Hypergeometric designs such as Proposition F.7.1, or the betting tests of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas (2020) in each stratum, stay valid there. Power is computed at exact restoration. Demanding it for every restored law within $\gamma$ of the same live law needs more than $\zeta'>0$: with $S=1$, $q_0=0$, $m=1$, $p=0.2$, $\gamma=0.1$, and $q^\star=0.35$, a restored rate of 0.3 fits both the live rate 0.2 and the unsafe live rate 0.4. If all outcomes of a stratum copy one latent bit, more executions add nothing, and the cost results do not apply. With $q_0$ and $w$ estimated, replacing them in $\mathcal A$ by simultaneous upper confidence limits of level $\alpha_{\log}$ enlarges $\mathcal A$ on the event of the limits. Running the test only when $q_0^U+\sum_sw_s^U\min\{1,\tilde p_s+\gamma_s\}<q^\star<q_0^U+\sum_sw_s^U$ for these limits $q_0^U$ and $w^U$, and otherwise not certifying, keeps the size at most $\alpha+\alpha_{\log}$, as in Remark F.8.4. Finally, runs from an earlier snapshot re-execute the agreeing steps (Remark F.10.2). Suppose they reach the first-deviation strata of a set $I$ with probabilities $w_s/\sum_{j\in I}w_j$ in every world, with the same restored harm laws and admissible rates; that their records can be simulated from the stratum and one restored harm bit by a randomization that does not depend on the world; and that they cost at least the mean cost of runs started at those deviations. Then data processing and the share splitting of Proposition F.9.1(v) show that restoring at the first deviations of Proposition F.10.1 never raises the bound of part (ii). The comparison does not cover earlier runs that observe more, restore differently, or route differently, and choosing coarser snapshots trades storage against the bound, which we do not solve.

### E7 Check (Post Hoc, Companion Material for Appendix F.9)

**E7 Check.** Take gate 2 of the two-gate run (Appendix D.3) at the declared ceiling $q^\star=0.25$, with the strata of its registered budgeted analysis: first blocks at steps 0 to 3, 4 to 7, and 8 or later. They hold 986, 879, and 661 unresolved continuations with 259, 129, and 31 harms ($p_s=0.263$, $0.147$, $0.047$) and 11.8, 8.2, and 3.6 recorded agent calls per continuation; the 69 first blocks at the last step leave no agent step, so the log settles them as harmless. Units the log settles as harmful, or cannot settle, have mass $q_0=0.100$, so $q=0.1585$ and $\zeta=0.0915$. At $\gamma_s=0$, $\alpha=0.05$, and $\beta=0.2$, Proposition F.9.1(ii) bounds the cost by 92.3 agent calls, or 11.9 executions at the optimal execution shares 0.31, 0.36, and 0.34. For executions in proportion to stratum size, the allocation of the registered design, the same argument gives 96.1 calls (11.4 executions). The pooled bound of Proposition F.9.1(iv) gives 12.2 executions, or 103 calls at the mean cost. Live testing needs at least 1,098 agent calls (77.3 episodes at 14.2 calls), so the leading cost ratio of Proposition F.9.2(iii) is 0.084. A live test that also trusts the log's masses and sees each episode's stratum gains $\sum_sw_s\mathrm{kl}(p_s,l_s)$ per episode against the nearest $l$ with $q_0+\sum_sw_sl_s\ge q^\star$, by the same change of measure, and needs at least 462 calls (32.6 episodes), a ratio of bounds of 0.20. At gate 1 and 0.30, a target of the registered budgeted curves, the optimal shares lower the bound from 169 to 155 calls. The optimal shares thus move the bound by 4% and 8%; the large factor, 5 to 12 here, is the one against live testing. These are lower bounds at the observed rates and mean costs, computed post hoc, not costs of a run.

### Relation to Prior Work

The bound of part (ii) and its saddle point follow the sequential design of experiments (Chernoff, 1959) and its characteristic time (Garivier and Kaufmann, 2016), which cost-aware best-arm identification states in cost shares (Kanarios et al., 2024). With unit costs and no restoration error, the saddle point of part (iii) is the half-space case of Juneja and Krishnasamy (2019, Theorem 5), whose least favorable alternative also equalizes the divergences. Multi-fidelity best-arm identification also pairs cheaper samples of known maximal bias with their costs (Poiani et al., 2024). Stratified sequential audits combine strata through union-intersection tests that stay valid under any stratum selection (Spertus and Stark, 2022; Spertus et al., 2024); the design-point test here uses one likelihood ratio, valid with its own allocation. What is specific here is the null set by restoration error with its cap at one and attached to the log's resolved mass, its solution by one scalar root with execution costs, and the comparison with live testing.

## Snapshot Benchmarks

### Count of Running Maxima

For exchangeable scores without ties, the $j$th decision after the boundary is a running maximum with probability $1/j$ (Rényi, 1962). So $n$ decisions have $H_n=\sum_{j\le n}1/j\approx\ln n+0.58$ expected running maxima, and at most $H_n$ above a floor. Probability theory calls them record values, which go back to Chandler (1952). For i.i.d. scores with an atomic law $\pi_v$ and $G(v^-)=P(m<v)$, the $j$th decision is a running maximum above $\theta_{\min}$ with probability $\sum_{v>\theta_{\min}}\pi_vG(v^-)^{j-1}$. A continuous part of the law adds terms, so the atomic formula undercounts there. Real scores differ by step, cluster, and repeat, so these values are benchmarks, and measured counts replace them (Appendix D.3).
